# A Note on My Work for Norfolk Botanical Gardens
*From Anton Bakker — first-person input for a journalist writing about the two installed pieces.
Reference artifact (not part of the public answer corpus). Companion to the on-site artist's
statements for Koos Knoopje and Reflection.*

---

I'm grateful that you're writing about the two pieces now at the gardens, Koos Knoopje and Reflection. The short artist's statements placed beside each work are meant for a visitor standing in front of the sculpture, so they stay brief. I wanted to give you something fuller here, in case it's useful as you write.

Both sculptures are paths traced through atomic lattice structures, the same invisible geometry that composes everything around them in the garden: the plants, the soil, the water, the visitor. A path moves through a set of points and, in these works, returns to where it began. That is the quiet idea underneath both pieces. Each is a journey made visible.

What I hope a person discovers, standing with the work, is a sequence I've come to trust. First there is beauty, the attraction we feel before we think. Most people are drawn to a flower without knowing why. I believe that pull is an innate recognition of symmetry. If you stay a little longer, you begin to see the pattern beneath the beauty, the repetition and balance that were always there. And if you stay longer still, you arrive at wonder, the deeper question of what is really going on here. Beauty, then pattern, then wonder.

The garden is the perfect place for this. People already do the work without naming it. They see a rose, they stop, they walk around it, they look from different angles. My sculptures simply make that act conscious. Once you notice that exploring from different positions is how you find beauty and balance, you can carry that practice beyond the gates and apply it to anything, including the things and the people you did not choose to encounter. Where we forget to explore each other's positions, symmetry tends to break. I think a great deal of our division comes from taking a side rather than looking at the sides.

Koos Knoopje is a figure eight knot rendered in flowing curves. As you circle it, your perspective changes the work completely. From one angle it stands upright; from another it appears inverted. A single motif, mirrored and then repeated four times, gives it a flower-like symmetry that I hope feels at home among the plantings.

Reflection is a Hamiltonian path, a route that visits every point of its structure once and closes back on itself. It is built in mirror-polished stainless steel and sits in its own pool, with benches nearby. The surface returns the garden, the sky, and the visitor. The piece was previously exhibited in Dubai in a larger reflecting pool alongside a sister sculpture that was acquired by the office of the UAE. In the rose garden, it stands for a simpler idea: life is a path of points, some chosen and many not, and the garden itself is only one point along a much longer journey.

If any of this is helpful, please use it freely. I'm happy to talk further.

Anton Bakker
