# Fence Around Nothing — the full story

The full record of Anton's Fence Around Nothing, the 5.5 m column, from his own account. Visitors
who come from its page have usually heard its short and deeper narrations; answer anything they ask
about it from here. Stay general about where exactly it stands until the placement is confirmed.

## The object
- **Mirror-polished stainless steel, 5.5 m tall, on a stainless steel disc**, for indoor display at
  Simac, in Veldhoven. It is on its way; **placement not yet confirmed**: hoped for in the welcome hallway
  next to a stairway, so visitors can experience it from different heights.
- **Two zigzags with the same rhythm, slightly shifted**, fitting together so tightly that they touch:
  the fence encloses nothing, not even air.
- **One closed path in the FCC lattice.**

## Origin
- Based on **Koos Verhoeff's zigzag sculpture, which he named Fence Around Nothing**: an **open** path
  of two zigzags in **round tube**, connected at the top in Koos's own fashion, with two strands left
  open at the bottom where it was fixed to a base.
- Anton placed it in the FCC lattice and searched for a **lattice-compliant closing at the top and the
  bottom**, making a closed path in keeping with his tradition.
- Anton's **profile** lets the zigzags fit together more tightly than Koos's round tube.
- To honour the start of the design by Koos, Anton keeps the name Koos gave it.

## Views
- It looks very different from different sides: a single zigzag line, a dual zigzag, or the other
  zigzag line.

## Meaning
- This piece is above all about its **name**: it brings up the whole subject of fences, raising
  questions and wonder.
- **Koos's joke:** people say they put a fence around their garden, but the neighbour may not like
  that, because you should put it in your own garden.
- Questions: is guarding even needed? Am I keeping things out, or keeping myself in?
