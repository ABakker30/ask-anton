# Quartangle — the full story

The full record of Anton's Quartangle, from his own account. Visitors who come from the Quartangle
page have usually heard its short and deeper narrations; this goes further, so answer anything they
ask about the piece from here.

## The object
- **Mirror-polished stainless steel, five feet (1.5 m) tall**, currently on public display.
- Sits on a **rectangular stainless steel base**.
- Made of **four identical equilateral triangles**, so the whole sculpture is built from **one
  identical edge, repeated twelve times**.
- The triangles are **paths in the FCC (face-centred cubic) lattice** — background, not a headline.
- Like all of Anton's sculptures: each path is **closed, never self-intersecting, and lies in a
  lattice**; he looks for paths with high symmetry. A triangle is one of the simplest such paths, so
  people recognise it easily.

## The linking and rigidity
- The triangles are **linked in pairs**. Each triangle passes through **only one** other triangle; no
  triangle goes through the other three.
- The **two pairs are not linked to each other**, yet together they constrain each other so that the
  structure is **completely rigid**: no triangle has any freedom of movement.
- **Remove any one triangle** and the others lose their rigidity. The strength is in the **set in
  combination**.
- Visitors should be able to see the linking and the rigidity for themselves.

## How it was found
- Anton did **not** set out to make this shape. He set out to explore the **simplest paths that can
  be linked**, how they can be linked, and whether some linkage shows remarkable beauty and other
  remarkable attributes such as rigidity.
- What made Quartangle stand out: its **compactness** (four triangles packed very tightly), the
  **symmetry of the packing**, and the **rigidity** created by two pairs constraining each other.

## Koos's three principles
- Anton's mentor **Koos Verhoeff** held three principles for art: **it should solve a problem, the
  solution should be beautiful, and when observed it should raise questions.**
- Anton: **"Symmetry is the architecture of beauty."** Where there is beauty — in flowers, in
  nature, even in a business transaction — there is likely symmetry or balance.

## Symmetry and perspective
- **180-degree rotational symmetry.**
- It also **appears to flip upside down with every 90-degree shift** in viewing position: from one
  angle it seems to stand on the feet of two triangles; from the next it seems to stand on its head.
- The cleanest flips: looking **square-on to the long side** of the base, and **square-on to the
  short side**.
- The mirror polish simply reflects the surroundings.
- Anton's point: two people seeing the same object from different sides see different things.
  Taking a position instead of exploring positions is the ingredient for an argument; people often
  argue because they **take a side rather than explore sides**.

## The questions a visitor may move through
1. What am I looking at?
2. I see a triangle. It's an equilateral triangle.
3. A second, a third, a fourth triangle.
4. All four triangles are identical.
5. So the whole sculpture is made of one identical stick.
6. Sticks form triangles, triangles form linked pairs, two linked pairs form the whole.
7. Why is it rigid?
8. What are its symmetries, and why does it flip upside down as I walk around it?

## Anton's philosophy of linkage
- His interest in linked objects comes from a deeper interest in life: **how everything is linked**
  — between people, businesses, corporations, governments, at every level. Life would be lonely if
  nothing were linked. Seeing how things are linked gives a deeper understanding of reality.
- Linking can mean **constraint, belonging, or both**, and that range is why links interest him.
- Does a family fall apart when one member is removed? A business structure? A political one? So
  many things depend on their linked partner.
- Exact fits and multiple linked pairs create sound, rigid structures, but they **remove degrees of
  freedom**. Good for a building that must not flex. But do we need to be careful not to take
  people's freedoms away?
- Anton could take this much further — into politics, business and more; this record is not the
  complete story.

## History
- Made as the **iconic sculpture for Bridges 2025 at TU Eindhoven**, hosted by Tom Verhoeff. It
  became the **conference logo**: seen square-on to the long side of the base, it looks like a
  **bridge**.
- Anton built his own **heuristic method for finding links in Grasshopper**. **Tom**, who chaired the
  conference (some **400 to 500 attendees** from around the world), worked hard to **rewrite Anton's
  Grasshopper component as a more efficient Python component**, and the two worked on it together.
  The structure was found with that code, though Anton had found it before. Anton felt it right to
  credit the piece as a **co-discovery by Tom and Anton: a collaboration**.

## Scale and uniqueness
- The FCC envelope Quartangle occupies has **hundreds of vertices**. Within it, the number of
  possible paths (different lengths, configurations, pairings and linkings) **exceeds the number of
  atoms in the visible universe**. Quartangle is one extraordinary configuration among them.
- Anton's analogy: **compute and algorithms are like ever-better telescopes, but humans still need
  to point them.** It still takes a human to ask the question.
