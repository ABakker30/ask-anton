# Ode to M.C. Escher — the full story

The full record of Anton's Ode to M.C. Escher, from his own account: the bronze of the linked
squares and the endless knot, earlier titled "Awakening". Visitors who come from its page have
usually heard its short and deeper narrations; answer anything they ask about it from here.
(Anton's other ode to Escher, Opus 548001, is a different sculpture.)

## The object
- **Bronze, about 10 inches tall**, on a fixed round base, in a large meeting room at Simac, in
  Veldhoven. Visitors walk around it. (A larger version, about 20 inches, also exists.)
- **One continuous closed path in the BCC (body-centred cubic) lattice**, which has **eight
  directions**, unlike the twelve of the FCC lattice used in pieces such as Quartangle and Clara.

## The three views
- **From the doorway: two linked squares** standing on their corners. An optical illusion: one square
  appears to float, though it does not. It is one line, not two squares.
- **A quarter of the way around: the endless knot** (the Tibetan or Buddhist endless knot), four loops
  woven around a centre.
- **In between, which is most of the views: a chaotic tangle** without the beauty of the other two.

## Why it is called Ode to M.C. Escher
- It is a nod to Escher's optical illusions. Escher influenced Anton's work throughout. (The Koos
  Verhoeff and Escher story is in escher-connection.)

## Meaning
- Illusions are made up in our heads: we take things for what we see, and it takes more thinking to
  realise what is there.
- It follows Koos's principle that a work should raise questions: how can this be two squares when
  one seems to float? And from the chaotic views: is it really this chaotic, or can we discover the
  beauty in things?
- The endless knot is a Buddhist symbol of interconnection, with no beginning and no end. Anton finds
  it meaningful that one object holds an illusion, a formless view and the endless knot.
- Anton considers it one of his most beautiful discoveries and a personal favourite.
- It sits in a meeting room: Anton imagines meetings moving through the same stages the sculpture
  shows, illusion, chaos and finally the connecting pattern, on their own subject.

## Quote
- **Silvia Lenaerts**, rector magnificus of Eindhoven University of Technology, in her lecture on
  Escher at Bridges 2025: "Look, and you will find beauty. Look deeper, and you will find patterns.
  Look yet even deeper, and you will experience wonder."
