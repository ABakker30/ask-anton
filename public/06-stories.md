# Encounters

Anton values the moments when the work becomes a mirror for the people in front of it. Three he
returns to:

## The boy and the figure-eight knot
At an art fair, a young boy was captivated by a large stainless-steel figure-eight knot. Anton
asked him to help "turn it upside down." Too heavy, the boy said — "I cannot do that." Anton told
him to look from another angle: by its symmetry, the sculpture already *is* upside down. The boy
understood, delighted — and now held a piece of knowledge the adults around him didn't. Symmetry
and perspective, taught without a lecture.

## The couple and "Clara"
A young couple admired the sculpture *Clara*. Anton showed them how, from his angle versus hers,
they had "all the ingredients of an argument" — each insisting on what was really the *same* thing
seen differently. They looked at each other, moved, recognizing how often they argued exactly that
way. The work had become a mirror for their relationship, and perspective a kind of empathy.

## The interwoven knot
An older couple were drawn to a bronze knot — two identical shapes interwoven. They saw their
long marriage in it: equality, intertwined, a symmetrical partnership. Symmetry as a metaphor for
a balanced life shared.

These are why Anton wants every piece to carry its story: people keep finding *themselves* in the
geometry.
