# Fractal Tree — the full story

The full record of the Fractal Tree, from Anton's own account. Visitors who come from its page have
usually heard its short and deeper narrations; answer anything they ask about it from here.

## The object (the one on view)
- **Mirror-polished stainless steel, 1.5 m tall**, on view at Simac, Veldhoven (atrium).
- Anton's own words (old website): "Each branch is an identical form, scaled and oriented in its own
  unique direction in space, due to the simple rule that generates the fractal. It shows how
  simplicity in form and rule can unfold into beauty and symmetry across every scale." A tribute to Koos.

## Structure (Anton, 2026-11-23)
- **Nine generations.** The count of branches keeps doubling: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and on.
- **The trunk is the exception** (Anton, 2026-11-23): it has an **extension that grounds the tree on its
  base**, so it is not exactly the same form as the other pieces. Word it as "with the exception of the trunk".
- Every branch is the same form at a smaller scale. What makes each one **unique is its direction in
  space**: "each one different" must be said clearly as being about direction.
- **Infinitely many unique directions** (Anton, 2026-11-23): this tree has infinite unique directions.
  If the branches alternated in orientation instead, the resulting tree would have **only 3
  directions**. That choice is what makes every branch's direction unique.
- **Symmetry at scale** (self-similarity), and a **180-degree rotational symmetry about its vertical
  axis**.
- **Fractals are the exception** to Anton's traditional closed paths in lattice structures. This should
  be pointed out.
- Anton has made **a few other fractal structures** (e.g. the "Diamond Fractal" shown at MoMath, the
  octahedral fractal "Desert Diamond").
- Construction (earlier notes): a 3D Pythagorean tree on the 1×√2 profile; a 45° cut gives one large
  square at the bottom, the opposite cut two small squares on top; copies scaled ~0.7 go on those.

## History
- Koos's design. Koos and Anton built the **first one together, in bronze, shown in New York in 1992**.
- The **2012 four-foot patinated bronze** ("Koos fractal tree", Bridges best in show 2012) is a
  **different tree from the 1992 one** (Anton, 2026-11-23).
- Later: mirror-polished stainless (1.5 m), a Corten version in progress, a monumental one envisioned
  for the Norfolk Botanical Gardens.

## Koos, the lecture, and what stayed with Anton (Anton, 2026-11-23)
- The wisdom of Koos's lecture **"Chaos and the Computer"** has stayed with Anton through life, and led
  to Anton translating it into English.
- The lecture was first given at a time when Koos was thinking about **automation**; Koos said that
  **one should not automate faster than is socially acceptable**. Anton thinks about this when
  contemplating the implications of **AI**.
- Another reason Anton likes fractals: **utter simplicity generating such beauty**. Koos had said that
  **simplicity shows remarkable dedication**. Anton's own belief: **complexity may be hiding simplicity
  yet to be discovered.**
