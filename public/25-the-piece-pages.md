# The Works on Their Own Pages (marble and steel, 2026)

A group of Anton's sculptures each has its own page, opened from a QR code on the plinth: a
visitor standing in front of a work scans the code and gets photographs, a short film, the story,
and a recording of that story read aloud. Each page also links here, to Ask Anton. Visitors who
come from those pages are usually standing in front of one of the works below, so when they ask
about "this piece," this is the group they mean.

The pages open with the story, not the mathematics, and end on the same idea: symmetry is what
makes a form feel right; perspective is what makes it surprising. *Look at all sides before you
pick a side.*

## Koos Knoopje (Carrara marble, 50 cm)
A white **Carrara marble** version, 50 cm tall, of **Koos Knoopje** — the figure-eight knot Koos
Verhoeff first made in straight segments and Anton was the first to render as a flowing curve,
naming it "Koos's little knot." It is the same form as the eight-foot red steel original and the
five-foot bronze at the Norfolk Botanical Gardens, now in stone at a table-top scale. Walk a
quarter of the way around it and the knot appears to turn upside down; nothing has moved except
the viewer.

## Clara (Carrara marble, 1 m and 50 cm)
**Clara** is a design of **two joined spirals**. It exists in **Carrara marble** in two sizes: a
large Clara, **one metre** tall, and a smaller Clara, **50 cm**, the same design at half the size.
- **The name:** Anton's partner Rebecca, a pianist, calls her Steinway "Clara," after Clara
  Schumann, and nothing may be set on it. Nothing, until a small bronze Clara: it was found worthy
  of the piano, and so the sculpture became Clara.
- **The couple:** at an art fair in the Hamptons, a young couple stood on either side of a Clara.
  From his side and from hers they had "all the ingredients of an argument" — each seeing
  something different, both right. They recognized themselves, and took the sculpture home.

## Infinity (pink Portuguese marble, 50 cm)
A sculpture in **pink Portuguese marble**, 50 cm tall. From one angle it is a perfect **infinity
sign**: one unbroken loop folding back on itself. From almost any other angle it becomes something
strikingly, puzzlingly different, and it is hard to believe it is the same stone. Both views are
true; neither is the whole story. (Its working title is "Infinity.")

## Quartangle (stainless steel)
Four triangles, linked so that none can be pulled free, yet none touches another. Anton found it
while searching for **links** — separate paths that are entangled yet fit snugly together. His
first search tool was a hand-tuned sketch; Tom Verhoeff turned it into rigorous mathematics. The
Quartangle was one of the finds, and in 2025 it became the **logo of the Bridges conference** on
mathematics and art. This one is realized in mirror-polished stainless steel.

## Fractal Tree (mirror-polished stainless steel)
Koos Verhoeff's **fractal tree** in mirror-polished stainless steel. Every branch is the same
shape, only smaller: one trunk becomes two branches, then four, eight, sixteen. Yet no two branches
point the same way; each has its own direction in space. Like people: alike at first sight, and
each one different. Koos and Anton built the first one together.

## Ode to M.C. Escher (bronze)
One bronze, three truths. From one side it is **two linked squares**; walk on and it becomes the
**Tibetan endless knot**, a single line with no beginning and no end; walk further and it closes
into a **cube**. Nothing about the object changes, only where you stand. At the **Museum of
Mathematics (MoMath)** in New York it turned slowly in a corner between two lights, casting two
different shadows on two walls at once: a nod to **M.C. Escher**, who made the impossible look
ordinary, and to **Plato's cave**, where we take a shadow for the whole truth.

**Name:** this is the **same sculpture** that was earlier presented as **"Awakening."** Anton now
calls it **"Ode to M.C. Escher"** (Opus 548001); use that name, and mention "Awakening" only as its
earlier title.
