# The Works on Their Own Pages (marble and steel, 2026)

A group of Anton's sculptures each has its own page, opened from a QR code on the plinth: a
visitor standing in front of a work scans the code and gets photographs, a short film, the story,
and a recording of that story read aloud. Each page also links here, to Ask Anton. Visitors who
come from those pages are usually standing in front of one of the works below, so when they ask
about "this piece," this is the group they mean. The works are on view at **Simac, in Veldhoven**
(Brainport Eindhoven region); see "The Region: From Escher to the Chip".

The pages open with the story, not the mathematics, and end on the same idea: symmetry is what
makes a form feel right; perspective is what makes it surprising. *Look at all sides before you
pick a side.*

## Koos Knoopje (Carrara marble, 50 cm)
A white **Carrara marble** version, 50 cm tall, of **Koos Knoopje** — the figure-eight knot Koos
Verhoeff first made in straight segments in one lattice (BCC). Anton discovered the same knot in
another lattice (FCC) and curved it; Koos loved the curved shapes, so Anton named this first curved
knot "Koos's little knot." The full story is under "Koos Knoopje — the full story". It is the same form as the eight-foot red steel original and the
five-foot bronze at the Norfolk Botanical Gardens, now in stone at a table-top scale. Walk a
quarter of the way around it and the knot appears to turn upside down; nothing has moved except
the viewer.

## Clara (Carrara marble, 1 m and 50 cm)
**Clara** is a **spiraling trefoil knot**: from one side it may look like a single spiral, from
another like two, and at certain viewpoints even up and down seem to reverse. It exists in
**Carrara marble** in two sizes: a large Clara, **one metre** tall, and a smaller Clara, **50 cm**,
the same design at half the size.
- **The name:** Anton's partner Rebecca, a pianist, calls her Steinway "Clara," after Clara
  Schumann, and nothing may be set on it. Nothing, until a small bronze Clara: it was found worthy
  of the piano, and so the sculpture became Clara.
- **The couple:** at an art fair in the Hamptons, a young couple stood on either side of a stainless steel Clara.
  From his side and from hers they had "all the ingredients of an argument" — each seeing
  something different, both right. They recognized themselves, and took the sculpture home.

## Infinity (pink Portuguese marble, 50 cm)
A sculpture in **pink Portuguese marble**, 50 cm tall. From one angle it is a perfect **infinity
sign**: one unbroken loop folding back on itself. From almost any other angle it becomes something
strikingly, puzzlingly different, and it is hard to believe it is the same stone. Both views are
true; neither is the whole story. (Its working title is "Infinity.")

## Quartangle (stainless steel)
Four identical equilateral triangles, linked in two pairs so that the whole is completely rigid,
yet no triangle touches another. Made for **Bridges 2025** at Eindhoven University of Technology, where it became the
**conference logo** (square-on to the long side of the base, it looks like a bridge). Its structure
was **co-discovered by Anton Bakker and Tom Verhoeff**: Anton's Grasshopper link-finder, rewritten
by Tom as Python. Mirror-polished stainless steel, 1.5 m. The full story is under "Quartangle — the
full story".

## Fractal Tree (mirror-polished stainless steel)
Koos Verhoeff's **fractal tree** in mirror-polished stainless steel. Every branch is the same
shape, only smaller: one trunk becomes two branches, then four, eight, sixteen. Yet no two branches
point the same way; each has its own direction in space. Like people: alike at first sight, and
each one different. Koos and Anton built the first one together.

## Fence Around Nothing (mirror-polished stainless steel column, 5.5 m) — coming soon
A zigzag of mirror-polished steel that rises **five and a half metres** and encloses nothing at all.
The name is **Koos Verhoeff's**: he made an **open** zigzag design and called it *a fence around
nothing*. Anton continued from Koos's design, exploring the lattice for a version that lies in the
lattice and forms a **closed path**. To honour where it began, he still calls the closed path by
Koos's name. Ask what people build fences around and, often enough, there is nothing inside — only
the fence itself. Another fence around nothing,
**Red Zig Zag**, stands fourteen feet tall in red steel in Norfolk, Virginia. This column has been
made and is on its way; where exactly it will stand is not yet confirmed. The full story is under
"Fence Around Nothing — the full story".

## Ode to M.C. Escher (bronze, the linked squares / endless knot)
One bronze, three views. From one side it is **two linked squares**, one of which seems to float; a
quarter of the way around it becomes the **Tibetan endless knot**, a single line with no beginning
and no end; and in between, for most of the way, it is a **tangle** with no clear shape. The one on
view at Simac is about 10 inches tall, in a large meeting room. The full story is under "Ode to M.C.
Escher — the full story". Nothing about the object changes, only where you stand. At the **Museum of
Mathematics (MoMath)** in New York it turned slowly in a corner between two lights, casting two
different shadows on two walls at once: a nod to **M.C. Escher**, who made the impossible look
ordinary, and to **Plato's cave**, where we take a shadow for the whole truth.

**Name:** first titled **"Awakening"**; now **"Ode to M.C. Escher."** Its story is told in full
under "Ode to M.C. Escher" in the stories.

## Two odes to Escher
Anton has made **more than one ode to M.C. Escher**, and both are optical illusions:
- the bronze above — two linked squares, a tangle and the endless knot (earlier titled "Awakening");
- **Opus 548001** (2021, patinated bronze, about 10 inches / 25 cm) — an illusion of a
  **cloverleaf (trefoil) knot made of right angles**, which can also appear to be two independent
  trefoil knots. Selected perspectives of Opus 548001 were shown as wall images at MoMath's
  "Alternative Perspective."

When a visitor asks about "the Ode to M.C. Escher," it is usually the first one if they are standing
in front of it (it is the one with its own page); if it is unclear, say that there are two and
describe both briefly.
