# The Spine, and Rekenen versus Tekenen

Two entries on the ideas behind the work. They sit alongside the regional entry (From Escher to the
Chip). They are the thinking, not the plans.

# Entry 1: The spine

The spine is the backbone idea that makes this one story rather than a set of coincidences. One
structure, the lattice, and the mathematics of symmetry that lives in it, runs from Escher's prints to
the silicon chip, and now, through computing, into the artist's hands. The claim is **shared
structure, not influence**: these people were looking at the same mathematics, in parallel, across a
century.

## The order of the argument
Anton builds the spine in this order, on purpose:
1. **New tools.** Computing, chips, the machines of the Brainport region, and AI. Together they are a
   kind of telescope, an instrument for reaching what the unaided mind cannot.
2. **New spaces.** Those tools let us explore spaces that were out of reach before, and the spaces are
   vast.
3. **Mathematics is the thread.** It builds the tools, and it is what the spaces are made of. Symmetry
   lives there.
4. **Discovery.** In those mathematical spaces we find the beauty of symmetry, and nature has that
   same symmetry. The forms are not invented. They were always there. The art is the process of
   finding them.

## The lattice as the through-line
The concrete form of the spine is the lattice, traced through each stage:
- **Escher.** His flat tessellations come from a two-dimensional lattice, and his space-filling
  prints, such as *Cubic Space Division*, are three-dimensional lattices. He kept a lattice made of
  metal wire on his desk and worked from it. So the lattice runs through both his 2D and his 3D work.
- **Koos Verhoeff.** A mathematician and philosopher at the Mathematisch Centrum in the early days of
  Dutch computing, he carried the lattice into sculpture and explored it for a lifetime.
- **Anton Bakker.** He finds closed paths through the lattice and casts them as sculpture.
- **The chip.** Silicon is a crystal lattice, and a chip's circuits are patterned on grids. The
  machines that do the patterning are built in the Brainport region.
- **AI.** The vector spaces and weights that modern AI runs on are lattice-like structures too.

Discipline on this point: the lattice is the deep structure of the enabling tools, and of matter
itself. It is not a style the art has to wear. This is not "lattice art."

## Why the lattice is fundamental
- Matter is built from the lattices of atoms. Nothing in the world would exist without them.
- The rule is trivial, a set of fixed directions on a regular grid, yet the number of forms it allows
  runs past counting. Even a short closed path opens combinations on the scale of the atoms in the
  universe. Simple to state, inexhaustible to explore.
- The same fixed lattice carved into silicon yields every chip there is. One alphabet, endless
  meaning. Constraint is what makes it possible, not what limits it.
- For the first time, our tools reach down to the scale at which matter is organized. Lithography
  patterns at the atomic grid, computing runs on it. Earlier tools, the lever and the engine, extended
  our muscles. These extend the mind.
- The guard that keeps this honest: the machine can search, but only a human can point it at beauty
  and recognize it when it appears. What stays irreducibly human is the pointing.

## Koos's three principles, as a loop
Credited to **Koos Verhoeff and his son Tom Verhoeff**. A work should: first, **solve a problem**;
second, **be beautiful**; third, **raise a question** in the viewer, the feeling of "how is this
possible?" The three form a loop rather than a list. A question is posed as a problem, the work
answers it by finding the form, and the finished form throws a new question back at the viewer. That
is the same everyday gesture as asking a question and getting an answer that leads to the next
question.

A viewer often walks the same descent the maker did: you look and see beauty, you look deeper and find
structure, you keep looking and feel wonder. Wonder is the "how is this possible?" at the end of that
path.

## Symmetry and perspective, the two pillars
- **Symmetry is the architecture of beauty.** It is why a discovered form holds together and stands.
- **Perspective** is why the discovery has to become a physical object, seen from many sides, rather
  than a flat image on a screen. A form that changes as you walk around it can only be met in three
  dimensions.

## Core lines
In Anton's own words:
- Forms are discovered, questions are composed.
- Beauty exists in vast spaces; the tools can search the space, if the human can sense where to look.
- The same instrument can be pointed at function or at beauty. The region's machines point their
  search at function. Anton points his at beauty.

Pioneers to credit: M.C. Escher and Koos Verhoeff.

# Entry 2: Rekenen versus tekenen

Two Dutch words that differ by a single letter: ***tekenen***, to draw, and ***rekenen***, to
calculate or reckon. The near-rhyme makes them a natural pair, and a clean way to name two ways of
making art, especially now that AI can make images.

## Anton's distinction
- **Tekenen**, in the age of AI, is producing an image from prompts and from learned pictures. It
  renders something that looks like what it was trained on. Most of what people call "AI art" works
  this way.
- **Rekenen** is deriving forms from stated rules inside a defined space, where a result can be
  checked right or wrong. A closed path either closes or it does not. Anton's work is rekenen, and he
  champions it as his approach.

The difference is not calculation replacing drawing. It is where the form comes from. A rekenen form
is discovered inside a structure, not rendered from examples. The rules define a vast space, and the
artist searches it for forms that are both correct and beautiful. The machine can search; only a human
can point it at beauty and recognize it. Forms are discovered, questions are composed.

## The bridge to Escher
Escher reached deep structure by hand, which is tekenen in the literal, older sense of the word. He
said he was no mathematician, yet he worked out hard structure by making, before the mathematicians
caught up. Rigour can come from making, not only from proof. The modern move extends that same spirit:
rekenen lets the artist explore spaces far too large for the hand, but the judgment that chooses a
form and recognizes its beauty is the same human act Escher was performing. (Paraphrase Escher; never
quote him.)

## The honest tension
This is Anton's view, and it is worth stating plainly because it is easy to get wrong.
- The formula, or the structure, is not the artwork. It is tempting, especially for mathematicians, to
  love the formula more than the form it makes. But a gallery visitor only ever sees the form.
- A useful way to hold it: the structure is the score, and the artwork is the performance. People
  enjoy music without reading the score, and nobody calls the score unimportant. The rekenen is how
  the work is made, and it becomes the story behind the work. The artwork itself is what people see.
- The viewer is the test, not the maker. A visitor should be able to feel that there is structure
  behind the work without being shown a single formula. A knot that flips as you walk around it does
  that on the spot.

## An on-ramp for non-mathematical visitors
Think of it as music for the eyes. Anton composes with the twelve directions of the lattice the way a
composer works with the twelve notes of a scale. The alphabet is fixed and given; the beautiful closed
paths are searched for, not invented. That is rekenen: a defined space, a human ear, and a form found
rather than made up.

## How to use these entries accurately
- **Do not name the movement.** Anton is deliberately holding off on a name until the spine is
  settled. If asked, say it does not have a name yet, and that this is on purpose.
- Keep to shared structure, not influence. Escher, Koos and Anton were drawn to the same mathematics
  across a century; one did not cause the next.
- The lattice connection is one cubic idea, not one identical lattice. Escher's space-filling print is
  a simple cubic lattice; silicon is a diamond cubic lattice. The idea is shared; the specific
  lattices differ.
- Do not quote Escher's own writings. Paraphrase.
- Present the stances as Anton's view. The championing of rekenen, and the claim that the formula is
  not the artwork, are his positions, not settled fact.
- The region builds the machines, not the chips, and not the AI. Keep this consistent with the
  regional entry.
