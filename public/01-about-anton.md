# About Anton Bakker

Anton Bakker is a sculptor who discovers and composes **lattice path forms**. His work explores
**symmetry as the architecture of beauty** and the **importance of perspective in understanding**,
not only in sculpture, but throughout life.

He composes **closed, non-self-intersecting paths** that travel through crystal lattices (simple
cubic, body-centered cubic, and face-centered cubic) and realizes them as sculpture in **stainless
steel and bronze**. He calls the work **"music for the eyes."**

He is not a figurative artist. In his own words, "I cannot draw, I cannot paint." His medium is the
**interesting shapes that live inside the lattice world** — beauty he believes already exists inside
the structure, waiting to be searched out and brought back.

What Anton loves is a deliberately **narrow space: the patient exploration of the crystal lattices**,
worked by hand and eye rather than by formal theory. Much of that exploration happens in
**Grasshopper**, the visual programming environment for the 3D modeling software Rhinoceros, where he
builds and searches his paths. When a problem needs deeper technical formalism, he leans on
collaborators like **Tom Verhoeff**.

Anton had been working in this lattice space since the **1980s** — but for decades it was a private
practice alongside another life. Before devoting himself fully to sculpture, he spent a career in
technology: he founded a data-mining company, which at one point collaborated with Niantic on the
mobile game *Ingress* — the geolocation technology that led to *Pokémon Go*. It was only after the
death of his mentor **Koos Verhoeff in 2018** that Anton decided to **take the work public** — a way
to keep Koos alive. He sold the business and turned to art full-time. As he puts it, "Now I do
**data-mining for beauty**." The transition was a natural one: "I am at home in the cubic lattice
space." The architectural way of thinking he learned in technology — separation of concerns, clean
interfaces, the willingness to start over — runs straight through the art.

Anton sees himself less as an inventor than a finder: "Sometimes I feel I'm **more of a discoverer
than a sculptor**." What the work reveals, he says, is "the beauty that exists at the **atomic
level**."

Anton's work is shown internationally, including a presence in the Middle East, where the geometric,
symmetrical character of the work sits naturally alongside the **Islamic geometric tradition**.

He often reminds people that a finished sculpture is more than an object: it carries a story.
"They'll see an **Anton piece**."
