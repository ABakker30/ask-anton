# Materials and Making

Anton works primarily in **stainless steel** and **bronze**, at scales that reach roughly eight
feet.

Early in his career he built a small **bronze foundry** and cast with the **lost-wax / ceramic
shell** method, shaping patterns and casting them in bronze. The stainless pieces are
metal-fabricated.

The physical form follows the path's execution (see *How the Work Is Made*): **polylinear**
pieces keep the sharp straight edges of the lattice, while **curved** pieces flow smoothly
through the lattice vertices.

A lineage note: Anton's mentor worked largely in **wood** — table saw and glue — which favored
straight, polylinear forms. Anton moved the work into **metal** and added the curved executions.

## A widening palette
Over time the materials have grown into a range, each with its own character:
- **Bronze** — warm and ancient.
- **Stainless steel**, including **mirror-polished** pieces that catch the light and the
  surroundings, and shift as you move around them.
- **Painted steel and aluminum** — vivid colour that reads from a distance (the red figure-eight
  knot at the Norfolk garden is painted steel).
- **Corten steel** — which weathers to a warm rust patina, letting a piece age and settle into its
  setting.
- **Marble** — taken up in recent years; the most classical and permanent of all, and the most
  demanding, since the symmetry has to be cut with great precision.

He also explores **shaped polymer finished with an electroplated metal coating**, which can be
**coloured** — useful for pieces made of several linked paths, where giving each path its own colour
lets you *see* the relationship at a glance.

## Gold, at the smallest scale
In the **1990s** Anton was an early pioneer of **casting jewellery from 3D printing**, well ahead of
its time. He has made about a dozen pieces in **gold**, one even set with stones along the edge of
the form. Worn as a pendant, a lattice path turns and reveals different shapes as you move — the
same play of perspective as the large sculptures, but intimate and close. He keeps these rare and
unique rather than mass-produced.

## From a render to a piece
Anton discovers and **renders his forms digitally first**, then chooses which ones deserve to become
physical work — not everything found in the search gets made. The renders also let people preview
pieces before they're fabricated.
